# Make the research useful to a Shopify learner

Read this before synthesizing an intake, growth note or HTML digest. The product is a learning Brain: teach the reader how a real business works and what they can try. Evidence discipline supports that explanation; it is not the subject of every paragraph. These are editorial checks, not a visible scoring form or extra payload.

## Start with a person, a situation and a product

Explain who pays, who uses/receives the product, what triggers the purchase and what arrives. Separate stated audiences from verified customers, but do not hedge an observable offer into an unreadable sentence. Prefer “官网面向家长，提供按儿童画定制的白色摆件” over repeated “尚未证实实际客群”. Describe one tangible scene; if it is your proposed example rather than an observed customer, say “例如可以测试…”. A hypothetical scene is allowed; it is not evidence of demand or the user's chosen niche.

Then ask what the buyer could do instead. A saved original, an ordinary product, a manual workaround or doing nothing can be a useful comparison without live market research. Label these as comparison options, not verified competitor offers. To claim a competitor exists or an underserved market, inspect a source. When a concrete selection question would materially benefit, do one bounded follow-up: typically one directly relevant alternative and one user discussion, or at most three additional pages. Keep the result in the same learning session. This is optional depth, not a mandatory search quota, full market scan, supplier search or growth investigation. With offline evidence, name what remains to be compared rather than inventing results.

Use judgment proactively: when the learner is exploring products and the case's main lesson depends on a pain point, differentiation or willingness to pay, do that small comparison if web tools and suitable sources are available. Do not wait for the exact words “深入研究”. Skip it when the user asks for a quick/source-only summary, evidence already answers the question, or the learning is specifically about storefront execution rather than product opportunity. A failed/empty check yields a short gap, not endless searching. Report actual scope so readers can distinguish richer research from clearer writing on the same inputs.

## Turn observations into usable practices

Each method should have a visible detail, a buyer problem it might help, and a small adaptation. Naming a concept alone is insufficient:

| Too abstract | Concrete learning |
| --- | --- |
| 保留参与，提升归属感 | 商家做完难的制作步骤，把上色留给孩子。可比较“自己上色”和“直接送出完成品”两种交付。 |
| 区分购买任务与交付边界 | 家长买给孩子一起做，与替孩子寄给长辈，是两种订单。产品页写明收到白坯还是上色成品，并分别说明制作与运输时间。 |
| 保留可识别特征 | 如果孩子最在意不对称的眼睛，预览时先确认这一点，再决定哪些细节必须为生产调整。这是拟议测试例子，不是已核验成品。 |

Do not reuse these Doodle2Life topics for unrelated brands. The transferable requirement is specificity: a named person, object, page element, decision or action that comes from the current case.

## Explain the studied category before transferring the lesson

“Category” means the studied brand's own product category and its interesting product/category move. It is not shorthand for the user's future product, general feasibility questions or implementation risks. Even when the user names a target idea, explain the case in this order:

1. **What category is this?** Name it in ordinary words and give actual product examples. Explain the parent product or activity when relevant. “Water-bottle decoration and small storage accessories: adhesive hooks, charms, straw toppers” is clearer than “expression devices on existing carriers”. Use the brand's visible offer rather than inventing a formal taxonomy.
2. **What is interesting or different within that category?** State the ordinary product role and what this case adds/recombines: a new use, buyer, occasion, product relationship, format, service or buying reason. Point to concrete evidence. For example, water-bottle accessories can bring wearable-accessory styling and small storage to a bottle the customer already carries. Interpret the category move without claiming the brand invented the category, broke all precedent or caused sales success.
3. **Only then, what can the learner borrow?** End with one transferable principle when useful, then move the user's proposed products, candidate technologies, experiments and constraints to a clearly labeled application/ideas section. Do not make the category explanation mostly about a different category the user wants to enter.

Category titles and opening summaries should identify the studied category and its distinctive angle. The reader should be able to answer “它卖哪一类东西？这个案例把原来的产品逻辑改了哪里？” before seeing their own future idea. In an HTML card, use the summary for category identity and the points for case mechanisms/evidence; an optional final takeaway can bridge to ideas. Do not open with the learner's use case or a list of burdens. In a shared wiki category page, keep the common scope but make each case's category role clear before its application. This is an explanation order, not three mandatory headings, three required innovations or a new-page-per-brand rule.

For category learning, also choose the relevant questions: why buy instead of a simpler substitute; how often the situation happens; who pays versus who uses; willingness to pay; customization; size/shipping; manual work; visual storytelling. Tradeoffs support the explanation after the reader understands the category and its highlight. Expand only what sources support or clearly frame as a question. If material is thin, name the observed category and say what is interesting but unresolved; never manufacture innovation. These lenses are not a scorecard or default US/EU/1688 preferences.

## Make current-case ideas concrete

An idea can be a concrete hypothesis without being a business commitment. Instead of only asking “参与是否带来价值”, specify a test scene and choice: “同一张获授权画作，一版由家庭自己上色，一版交付完成品；有送礼日期的家长会选哪种，为什么？” Explain what answer would change the hypothesis. Preserve the current-case-first idea rule; do not force a fresh product, arbitrary audience or old-project application. Thin material can still yield no standalone idea.

## Teach growth without turning the case into an audit

Keep the useful distinction between observed tactics and your proposals. Low follower counts, blocked posts and identity exclusions answer evidence questions; summarize them briefly in the growth section and put detailed query/account notes in raw or the growth source footer. They are not the case's headline or its winning formula.

If actual channel evidence is sparse, say what is known once, then explain a source-grounded expression the learner could test, such as a concrete demonstration format or a page's buying question. Label this as a proposal, never as the brand's tactic or proof of success. Do not manufacture a full channel plan to fill three boxes. Research hygiene (“confirm identity first”) can be a short note when useful, but should not displace marketing/content learning.

## Edit for a reader, then check truth

Use short Chinese sentences and concrete nouns/verbs. Explain terms such as “归因”“承接”“可识别性” through an example or replace them. Lead headings with the learning, not the research process: “用同一张画展示变化” is more informative than “机制尚待验证”. Put shared limits once in the final note; keep a nearby qualifier when its absence would turn a proposal into a fact. Do not remove uncertainty merely to make the story attractive.

Before saving, silently check: can a new reader explain the offer and category; identify the case's specific category highlight before any application; point to evidence behind each method; compare a meaningful alternative; describe a current-case idea or its real evidence gap; and take one specific next step? If the category still makes sense only as advice for the user's future product, rewrite it around the studied brand first. If removing the brand's name makes the report fit any website, rewrite the generic parts. Check that no competitor, metric, audience or completed action was invented. Mechanical tests and hashes do not certify this editorial quality.
